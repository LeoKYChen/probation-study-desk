Andrew Carnegie's Life and Philanthropy

文章
第 1 段Andrew Carnegie, once the world’s richest person, was born in 1835 to a weaver’s family in Scotland. As a child, he was expected to follow his father’s profession. But the industrial revolution destroyed the weavers’ craft, and the family had to leave for new possibilities in America.
第 2 段In 1848 the Carnegies arrived in Pittsburgh, then the iron-manufacturing center of the country. Young Carnegie took odd jobs at a cotton factory and later worked as a messenger boy in the telegraph office. He was often asked to deliver messages to the city theater, where he would stay to watch plays by great playwrights. He also spent most of his leisure hours in a small library that a local benefactor made available to working boys.
第 3 段After the Civil War, Carnegie saw great potential in the iron industry. He devoted himself to the replacement of wooden bridges with stronger iron ones and earned a fortune. He further introduced a new steel refining process to convert iron into steel. By 1900, Carnegie Steel produced more of the metal than all of Great Britain.
第 4 段However, Carnegie often expressed his uneasiness with the businessman’s life. Wishing to spend more time receiving instruction and reading systematically, he once wrote, “To continue much longer overwhelmed by business cares and with most of my thoughts wholly upon the way to make more money in the shortest time, must degrade me beyond hope of permanent recovery.” The strong desire for intellectual pursuit led him to sell his company and retire at 64.
第 5 段Fond of saying that “the man who dies rich dies disgraced,” Carnegie then turned his attention to giving away his fortune. He abhorred charity; instead, he used his money to help others help themselves. He established over 2,500 public libraries, and sponsored numerous cultural, educational and scientific institutions. By the time he died in 1919, he had given away 350 million dollars.

逐段中譯
第 1 段安德魯·卡內基曾經是世界首富，1835 年出生在蘇格蘭一個織布工的家庭。小時候，大家都期待他繼承父親的行業。但是工業革命摧毀了織布工的手藝，全家只好前往美國，尋找新的機會。
第 2 段1848 年，卡內基一家抵達匹茲堡，那裡當時是美國的煉鐵中心。年輕的卡內基在一家棉紡廠打零工，後來在電報局當送信童。他常常奉命把電報送到市立劇院，送完就留下來看大劇作家的戲。他也把大部分的空閒時間，花在一間小圖書館裡，那是當地一位捐助人開放給工作的少年使用的。
第 3 段南北戰爭以後，卡內基看出鋼鐵業的巨大潛力。他全心投入，用更堅固的鐵橋取代木橋，賺了大錢。他又引進新的煉鋼方法，把鐵煉成鋼。到了 1900 年，卡內基鋼鐵公司的鋼產量，比全英國加起來還多。
第 4 段然而，卡內基常常表示他對商人的生活感到不安。他希望多花時間接受教導、有系統地讀書，曾經寫道：「如果再這樣下去，讓生意上的煩惱壓得喘不過氣，滿腦子只想著怎麼用最短的時間賺更多的錢，我一定會墮落到永遠無法挽回的地步。」強烈的求知慾，讓他在 64 歲時賣掉公司退休。
第 5 段卡內基很喜歡說「死時富有的人，死得可恥」，退休以後，他就把心思放在捐出他的財富上。他憎惡施捨，而是用他的錢幫助別人自助。他設立了超過 2,500 座公共圖書館，也資助了無數文化、教育和科學機構。到了 1919 年過世的時候，他一共捐出了 3 億 5,000 萬美元。

字詞搭配 配對與接法
「字詞搭配」是要整組記的說法，分兩種：一種是哪兩個字配在一起，例如 pose a serious risk（造成嚴重的風險）、in unison（齊聲），換成意思相近的字就不自然；另一種是這個字後面要接什麼，寫成公式，例如 be resistant to + N（對……有抵抗力）。公式裡的 + N 表示接名詞，+ V 接原形動詞，+ V-ing 接動名詞，sb 指人，sth 指事物，「時間」「地方」這類中文表示那裡要填的東西。名稱不必背，看得出這一條寫的是「配對」還是「接法」就夠了；記住搭配和接法，這個字你才用得出來，不會只是看得懂。一看就懂的組合和專有名詞不列。每一條寫的是查字典時的基本形，文中的寫法不同時，下面附上原文。
be born to + 家庭出生在……的家庭第 1 段原文：was born in 1835 to a weaver’s family
follow one's father's profession繼承父親的職業第 1 段原文：follow his father’s profession
leave for + 地方動身前往……第 1 段原文：had to leave for new possibilities in America
take odd jobs打零工第 2 段原文：took odd jobs
make + sth + available to + sb讓某人可以使用……第 2 段原文：made available to working boys
see potential in + N看到……的潛力第 3 段原文：saw great potential in the iron industry
devote oneself to + N／V-ing全心投入……第 3 段原文：He devoted himself to the replacement
the replacement of A with B用 B 取代 A第 3 段原文：the replacement of wooden bridges with stronger iron ones
earn a fortune賺大錢第 3 段原文：earned a fortune
convert A into B把 A 轉變成 B第 3 段原文：to convert iron into steel
express one's uneasiness with + N表示對……感到不安第 4 段原文：expressed his uneasiness with the businessman’s life
lead + sb + to + V讓某人……第 4 段原文：led him to sell his company
be fond of + V-ing喜歡……第 5 段原文：Fond of saying
turn one's attention to + N／V-ing把注意力轉向……第 5 段原文：turned his attention to giving away his fortune
give away + N捐出……第 5 段原文：giving away his fortune
by the time + 子句到……的時候第 5 段原文：By the time he died in 1919
