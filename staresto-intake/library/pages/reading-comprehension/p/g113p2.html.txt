113 學測閱讀測驗：畫家 Thomas Moran 與黃石公園
Painter Thomas Moran and Yellowstone
學測 113年第 39–42 題4 題

文章
第 1 段Thomas Moran, a famous painter in the 19th century, played an important role in the establishment of American national parks. His vivid paintings brought the splendor of the extraordinary landscapes before the eyes of American people, thus setting the stage for the regions to be widely recognized and officially established as national parks.
第 2 段Moran came to the United States at age 7 with his family and settled in Philadelphia. They came from northwest England, the blackened heart of the Industrial Revolution: Its main street was “a dark, unattractive hole” and the river running through it was a string of dirty water. That was all the nature that Moran knew. Moran began painting by age 15, inspired by the landscape paintings of the British master J.M.W. Turner. There was plenty of landscape for him to paint in America, much different from his hometown. Showing great talent in painting, Moran was soon hired as an illustrator at Scribner’s Monthly, and later appointed chief illustrator by age 34. In 1871, he was appointed to illustrate The Wonders of Yellowstone, a story by Nathaniel P. Langford, who had participated in an expedition to Yellowstone. Captivated by the utterly fantastic sights Langford described, Moran became eager to see this odd territory for himself.
第 3 段In 1871 Moran joined the first US government survey of the Yellowstone region with photographer William Henry Jackson. For two weeks he filled his sketchbook with the landscape’s most stunning sights. Moran’s watercolors—the first color renderings of the area—as well as Jackson’s photos and the survey results were presented to the Congress. His powerful images of Yellowstone fired the imagination of Congress members. In March 1872, lawmakers officially made Yellowstone a national park, the world’s first.
第 4 段Before Moran arrived, Yellowstone in the popular imagination was a harsh, wild place with hot water and steam coming out of hellish holes in the ground. Since the painter’s work appeared, Yellowstone National Park has come to be known as a picturesque wonderland. By the time Moran died, he had painted a dozen other areas that would become national parks or monuments.

第 3 段Moran’s watercolors—the first color renderings of the area—as well as Jackson’s photos and the survey results were presented to the Congress.
同行的 Jackson 拍了照片，最早的彩色圖像卻是 Moran 的水彩畫，可見當時的照片不是彩色的。(B) 方向相反：他在 1871 年親自去黃石之前，已經在為黃石的故事畫插圖；(D) 照抄 watercolors，但文章沒有比較哪一種風景畫最流行；(A) 文章沒說那份雜誌在推廣國家公園。
逐段中譯
第 1 段Thomas Moran 是 19 世紀的知名畫家，在美國國家公園的設立上扮演了重要的角色。他生動的畫作讓美國人親眼看到那些非凡景觀的壯麗，為這些地區日後廣受肯定、正式成為國家公園鋪好了路。
第 2 段Moran 7 歲時隨家人來到美國，在費城定居。他們來自英格蘭西北部，那裡是工業革命燻得烏黑的核心地帶：大街是「一個又暗又難看的洞」，流經當地的河是一條髒水。這就是 Moran 所認識的大自然。Moran 15 歲就開始作畫，靈感來自英國大師 J.M.W. Turner 的風景畫。美國有大量和他家鄉截然不同的風景，可以讓他盡情作畫。Moran 展現了繪畫天分，不久就受聘為《Scribner’s Monthly》的插畫家，34 歲時升任首席插畫家。1871 年，他受命為 Nathaniel P. Langford 的〈黃石奇景〉繪製插圖，Langford 曾參加過黃石的探險隊。Langford 描述的奇幻景象深深吸引了 Moran，讓他迫不及待想親眼看看這片奇特的土地。
第 3 段1871 年，Moran 和攝影師 William Henry Jackson 一起參加美國政府第一次的黃石地區考察。兩個星期裡，他的素描本畫滿了當地最壯觀的景色。Moran 的水彩畫是這個地區最早的彩色圖像，連同 Jackson 的照片和考察結果一起呈交國會。他筆下震撼人心的黃石景象，激發了國會議員的想像。1872 年 3 月，國會正式把黃石設為國家公園，這是全世界第一座國家公園。
第 4 段在 Moran 去之前，一般人想像中的黃石是個荒涼的蠻荒之地，地上有冒著熱水和蒸氣、像地獄一樣的洞。這位畫家的作品問世以後，黃石國家公園才開始以如畫般的仙境聞名。到 Moran 過世時，他還畫了另外十二處後來成為國家公園或國家保護區的地方。

字詞搭配 配對與接法
「字詞搭配」是要整組記的說法，分兩種：一種是哪兩個字配在一起，例如 pose a serious risk（造成嚴重的風險）、in unison（齊聲），換成意思相近的字就不自然；另一種是這個字後面要接什麼，寫成公式，例如 be resistant to + N（對……有抵抗力）。公式裡的 + N 表示接名詞，+ V 接原形動詞，+ V-ing 接動名詞，sb 指人，sth 指事物，「時間」「地方」這類中文表示那裡要填的東西。名稱不必背，看得出這一條寫的是「配對」還是「接法」就夠了；記住搭配和接法，這個字你才用得出來，不會只是看得懂。一看就懂的組合和專有名詞不列。每一條寫的是查字典時的基本形，文中的寫法不同時，下面附上原文。
play an important role in + N在……扮演重要角色第 1 段原文：played an important role in the establishment of American national parks
set the stage for + N為……鋪路第 1 段原文：setting the stage for the regions to be widely recognized
be appointed + 職位受聘擔任……第 2 段原文：later appointed chief illustrator
participate in + N參加……第 2 段原文：had participated in an expedition to Yellowstone
be eager to + V很想……第 2 段原文：became eager to see this odd territory
see + sth + for oneself親眼看看……第 2 段原文：to see this odd territory for himself
fire the imagination of + sb激發某人的想像第 3 段原文：fired the imagination of Congress members
come to be known as + N後來以……聞名第 4 段原文：has come to be known as a picturesque wonderland
